Beginner Foundation

3D Printing 101: Key Concepts

For Students & Beginners

The MOD-t uses FDM (Fused Deposition Modeling) to construct 3D objects layer-by-layer from melted PLA filament string pushed through a heated nozzle.

STL File

The 3D digital model blueprint downloaded from sites like Thingiverse or made in Tinkercad. It describes the shape without printing details.

G-Code

The exact machine instructions generated by Cura. Tells the MOD-t where to move, how fast to go, and what temperature to set.

Layer Height

The thickness of each plastic layer (usually 0.1mm to 0.2mm). Thinner layers mean smoother prints but take longer time.

Infill (%)

3D prints are hollow inside with a mesh pattern. 15% infill is strong enough for toys, while 50%+ is for heavy mechanical parts.

Bed Adhesion

Techniques used to stick the first layer of plastic securely to the unheated MOD-t build tray (Skirt, Brim, or Raft).

Hotend / Nozzle

The heated metal tip at the print head (200°C / 392°F). Squeezes plastic out through a tiny 0.4mm hole.

Patented Motion System

How the MOD-t Printer & Bed Tray Works

2-Axis Pinion Mechanism

Unlike conventional 3D printers where the print head moves left, right, forward, and backward, the New Matter MOD-t uses an ingenious floating bed tray motion system. Understanding how this tray interacts with the printer gears is key to preventing job failures and grinding noises.

1. Dual Pinion Gear Rod Drive (X & Y Axes)

The print head nozzle inside the MOD-t only moves straight UP and DOWN (Z-axis). All horizontal X and Y motion is created by two rotating grooved gear shafts located beneath the bed platform.

  • The bottom of the motion carriage has perpendicular rack gear grooves molded directly into it.
  • Rotating the left rod shifts the tray diagonally; turning both in tandem creates precise X-Y positioning.
  • Because the tray is completely detachable, it sits directly on top of metal ball bearings.
2. Two-Part Build Tray Assembly

The build bed consists of two separate components that snap together:

  1. The Motion Base (Carriage): Heavy grey plastic base plate with bottom gear teeth that rides on the printer drive shafts.
  2. The Clear Build Plate (Tray Insert): Flexible clear plastic plate where plastic prints stick. It snaps securely into the top slots of the motion base.

Critical Requirement: Proper Bed Alignment & Seating Before Every Print

Pre-Print Checklist

Because the MOD-t bed tray is not fixed in a track with screws, if the tray is placed off-center by even a single gear tooth or tilted at an angle, the printer will misalign during homing. This results in loud grinding noises, off-center prints, nozzle scrapes, or instant motor stalls!

STEP 1
Snap Plate to Base

Ensure the clear plastic build plate is firmly pressed down and locked into all 4 corner tabs on the grey motion carriage.

STEP 2
Clear Gear Channels

Check underneath the metal gear rods for stray filament strands or plastic scrap bits that could block tray travel.

STEP 3
Orient & Center Tray

Place the base flat on the stainless steel gear rods so the bottom rack teeth engage square and parallel to the printer frame.

STEP 4
Gentle Press & Wiggle

Press down lightly on the center of the bed with two fingers and wiggle gently to verify the teeth are fully meshed in the drive gears.

Complete all 3 checks before clicking "Print" in the New Matter Utility.

Bed Adhesion: Skirt, Brim & Raft

Crucial for MOD-t Unheated Bed

Because the MOD-t features a non-heated plastic build tray, proper bed adhesion options in Cura are critical to prevent prints from slipping or warping off the bed during a print job. Below are the three primary types:

1. Skirt

No Direct Contact
Prime & Leveling Line

A single or double line printed on the build plate surrounding the model, without touching it.

  • Purpose: Primes the nozzle to ensure smooth plastic flow before printing the actual model, and allows you to check bed leveling.
  • Does NOT help: Stick models with small bases to the bed.
  • Best for: Large prints with plenty of surface contact area.
When to use: Standard default for large, flat-bottom models.

2. Brim

Attached Border
Anti-Warping Flange

A single-layer wide flat border attached directly around the base edges of your model (like the brim of a hat).

  • Purpose: Greatly increases the bottom surface area touching the build plate to prevent corners from curling/warping.
  • Easy to remove: Peels or snips off easily after the print finishes.
  • Best for: Tall models, sharp corners, or small footprint items on the MOD-t tray.
Recommended for MOD-t: Highly recommended default for most classroom prints!

3. Raft

Underneath Platform
Multi-Layer Substrate

A thick, multi-layer mesh raft printed directly onto the bed, and your entire model is printed on top of the raft.

  • Purpose: Provides maximum adhesion on uneven/scratched bed trays and ensures a flat starting plane.
  • Trade-off: Consumes extra filament and leaves a slightly rougher finish on the bottom surface of the print.
  • Best for: Models with extremely tiny footprints (e.g. figurine feet) or warped build plates.
When to use: Tiny contact points or problem surfaces that won't hold a brim.

Classroom Safety Rules for Students

Always review these rules before letting middle or high school students operate the MOD-t.

1. Hotend Warning

The metal nozzle reaches 200°C (392°F). Never touch the print head nozzle while heated or during printing!

2. Keep Cover On

Always keep the clear draft cover snapped over the MOD-t during operation to protect hands from moving rods and maintain warm air.

3. No Forceful Pulling

Never yank cold filament out backwards. Always heat the hotend or snip filament cleanly using side-cutters during filament reload.

Step-by-Step Lifecycle

The 6-Step Printing Workflow

From finding a 3D file on your Mac to holding the finished physical object.

1 Design / Find

Obtain an STL File

Download a 3D model (`.stl`) from Thingiverse.com or create your own custom model in Tinkercad.

2 Cura Slicer

Slice in Cura 15.04.6

Drag `.stl` into Cura. Set layer height (`0.2mm`), speed (`35mm/s`), temperature (`200°C`), and adhesion (`Brim`), then save as `.gcode`.

3 Preparation

Prep Build Tray

Wipe clear tray clean and ensure it is properly seated flat on the dual gear shafts. Apply purple glue stick or painter's tape for adhesion.

4 Filament

Load PLA Filament

Insert PLA into intake at 200°C, then press physical front button once it catches to extrude test strand.

5 USB Transfer

Send & Press Front Button

Load `.gcode` in New Matter Utility, click **Print**, wait for front LED button to flash, then **press button** to start!

6 Completion

Cool Down & Remove

Let nozzle cool down completely. Remove tray and gently flex plastic plate to pop the finished print off cleanly.

Filament Operation Walkthroughs

1 Loading Filament (Detailed App & Hardware Steps)

Temp: ~200°C
Where to Click in the MOD-t App:
  1. Open MOD-t Printer Utility on your iMac.
  2. Click on Settings (or the gear icon in the top right menu).
  3. Click on Load Filament.
  4. Wait for the app status to change from Heating... to Insert Filament.
  1. Trim the tip of your 1.75mm PLA filament cleanly at a sharp 45-degree angle using flush cutters.
  2. Guide the filament through the rear plastic tube down into the top opening of the extruder drive gear.
  3. Gently apply downward pressure on the filament line until you feel the motor gear grab the plastic tip.
  4. CRITICAL STEP: Look at the front of the printer. The physical front LED button will start flashing. Press the flashing front button on the printer once to confirm filament is loaded and start pushing a test strand out of the nozzle.
  5. Verify molten PLA extrudes smoothly in a straight strand from the nozzle, then click Done in the App window.

2 Unloading Filament ("No-Jam" Method for Students)

Avoid Gear Jams

Avoid the Unload command to prevent extruder jams from swollen filament tips. Follow the Purge & Snip routine below:

  1. In the app, click Settings $\rightarrow$ Load Filament (do not click Unload).
  2. Wait for hotend to reach 200°C and for motor gear to start driving forward.
  3. Press the flashing physical front button on the printer to begin extruding plastic downwards.
  4. Snip & Feed Action: While plastic is extruding out of the nozzle, use side-cutters to snip the filament line right above the top intake hole.
  5. The short remaining plastic stub will feed cleanly down out through the hotend without pulling swollen bulbs backward into gears!

3 Clearing Clogs & Extruder Jams

The Cold Pull (Atomic Pull) Method

Best for clearing debris, charred PLA, or severe partial nozzle clogs without disassembling the hotend.

  • Heat nozzle to 210°C and push filament in manually.
  • Let hotend cool down to ~90°C (PLA transitions to rubbery state).
  • Firmly pull filament straight up out of the top intake.
  • Examine tip: it should show a perfect inverse mold of the internal nozzle cavity containing debris.

Cleaning Extruder Gear & Heat Creep

If the extruder makes a "clicking" noise, filament is grinding against the brass drive gear.

  • Remove top plastic shield cover and inspect drive gear teeth.
  • Use a small stiff wire brush or needle to sweep out ground PLA plastic dust.
  • Check rear heatsink fan: if fan fails, heat creeps upward into PTFE tube, swelling filament prior to hotend entry.

Step-by-Step Installation

Installing both Definition Files & Print Profiles

Configuring Cura requires two distinct components from the tripflex/MOD-t repository: the Machine Definition File (tells Cura about the MOD-t's 150 × 100 × 125 mm physical size) and the Print Profile File (sets temperatures, speeds, layer heights, and retracts).

1 Install Machine Definition & Bed Model Assets

macOS / OS X Yosemite

On macOS, right-click (or Control-click) /Applications/Ultimaker Cura.app and select Show Package Contents. Navigate to the internal resources directories and copy the following files:

1. Place Machine Definition File:
/Applications/Ultimaker Cura.app/Contents/Resources/resources/definitions/modt.def.json
2. (Optional) Place 3D Print Tray Platform Preview:
/Applications/Ultimaker Cura.app/Contents/Resources/resources/meshes/modt_platform.stl
macOS Gatekeeper Fix: If macOS prevents Cura from launching after modifying internal package files, open the Terminal app and execute:
sudo xattr -cr '/Applications/Ultimaker Cura.app'
sudo xattr -cr '/Applications/Ultimaker Cura.app'

2 Add MOD-t Printer in Cura UI

  1. Launch Ultimaker Cura on your Mac.
  2. Open the Printer Selection Menu: Navigate to Settings $\rightarrow$ Printer $\rightarrow$ Add Printer... (or click the top left printer dropdown).
  3. Select Add a non-networked printer (or Other).
  4. Scroll to locate New Matter $\rightarrow$ select MOD-t and click Add.

3 Loading the Slicer Printing Profile (`.ini` or `.curaprofile`)

Tripflex Config File

Once the printer definition is created, you must import the calibrated tripflex print profile so Cura applies optimized feed rates, temperature targets (200°C), retract distances (2.5mm), and start/end sequences. Choose the method below based on your Cura version:

Method A: Cura 15.04.6 (Yosemite Default)
.ini format
  1. Download NM Cura Profile 151020.ini from the tripflex GitHub repo.
  2. In Cura menu, click File $\rightarrow$ Open Profile... (or Load Profile from File...).
  3. Browse to your Downloads folder and select NM Cura Profile 151020.ini.
  4. Verify that Print speed updates to 35 mm/s and Printing temperature updates to 200°C.
Method B: Cura 2.x / 3.x / 4.x Modern
.curaprofile format
  1. Download modt.curaprofile from the community repository.
  2. Click Preferences $\rightarrow$ Configure Cura... (or ⌘ + ,).
  3. Select Profiles on the left panel and click Import.
  4. Select modt.curaprofile, then click Activate to set as default.

4 Verify Start & End G-Code Sequences

Under Machine Settings in Cura, confirm that the Start and End G-Code scripts match the MOD-t homing routine:

Start G-Code:
G28 ; Home all axes
G1 Z5 F5000 ; Lift nozzle
M109 S200 ; Heat nozzle to 200C and wait
G92 E0 ; Reset extruder position
End G-Code:
M104 S0 ; Turn off hotend heater
G91 ; Relative positioning
G1 E-2 F300 ; Retract 2mm to prevent oozing
G1 Z10 F5000 ; Move hotend up away from print
G28 X0 Y0 ; Home X and Y
M84 ; Disable stepper motors

Visual Troubleshooting

Print Defect Visual Gallery

Identify common print issues visually and apply immediate fixes.

"Spaghetti" Nest

Lost Adhesion

Print detached from bed midway and extrudes plastic into mid-air.

Fix: Clean bed tray with isopropyl alcohol/water and apply purple glue stick or enable Brim in Cura.

Cobwebs / Stringing

Oozing

Thin hairs of plastic connecting gaps between object pillars.

Fix: Lower nozzle temp by 5°C (to 195°C) and ensure retraction is set to 2.5mm at 25mm/s.

Warping Corner Lift

Thermal Shrink

Bottom corners curl upward off the unheated plastic bed plate.

Fix: Use a 6mm Brim or Raft in Cura, and keep clear acrylic draft cover on the printer during print.
